<h1>Oboeru — 覚える</h1>

<h3>
A Japanese vocabulary learning app built for real consistency.
10 fresh N5 words every day. Spaced repetition that actually works.
A design you'll want to open.
</h3>

<p><em>覚える (oboeru) — to remember, to memorize</em></p>

</div>

<h1>Why Oboeru</h1>
<p>
Most flashcard apps fail not because of bad content — but because of bad design and too much friction. Anki works but feels like a chore. Duolingo gamifies but loses depth.
</p>
<p>
Oboeru was built to solve a personal problem — staying consistent with Japanese vocabulary when nothing else was working. The result is an app that removes every excuse not to open it: automatic daily decks, zero setup, and a warm Japanese aesthetic that makes studying feel like a ritual instead of a task.
</p>

<h1>Features</h1>
<ul>
<li>10 fresh N5 vocabulary words every day — automatically generated, never repeated</li>
<li>5 kana words + 5 kanji words — balances reading systems for solid foundations</li>
<li>Spaced repetition with 5-day fortification — new words appear daily for 5 days before spacing out</li>
<li>Card flip study session with spring animation</li>
<li>Got it / Forgot tracking — drives the spaced repetition intervals</li>
<li>Japanese dictionary search via Jisho API — search any word and save it</li>
<li>Personal library — All / Review Now / Mastered filters</li>
<li>Dedicated review session for saved and due cards</li>
<li>Full Core Data persistence — everything survives app restarts</li>
<li>Warm Japanese watercolor aesthetic — designed to be opened</li>
</ul>

<h1>The Learning System</h1>
<p>
Oboeru uses a modified spaced repetition algorithm designed specifically for vocabulary acquisition — not generic memorisation.
</p>
<p>
New words appear every day for 5 consecutive days to build a strong initial memory trace. After that, intervals grow progressively: 3 days, 7 days, then multiplied by an ease factor that adjusts based on your performance. Words you struggle with come back sooner. Words you know drift further apart.
</p>
